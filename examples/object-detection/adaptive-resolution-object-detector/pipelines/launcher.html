Adaptive Pipeline

Implementation

High-Scale

One shared detector for every stream

What it is

All cameras feed one detector graph. Video is forwarded to Insight untouched (no re-encoding), and only a decoded copy goes to the detector.

Why use it

  • Highest stream count — each extra camera costs only a decoder
  • Boxes stay accurate even at 16 streams
  • Lowest hardware cost per stream of the three

Trade-off

  • Adding or removing any stream restarts all of them (~10–30 s)
  • Not suited to cameras that come and go often

Capacity (30 fps)

Live-Add

A private pipeline per stream

What it is

Every camera gets its own complete chain — decoder, model, MLA and encoder — so the streams are genuinely independent of one another.

Why use it

  • Adding a camera does not restart the others — true live add
  • Best when cameras join and leave during a session

Trade-off

  • Most expensive per stream — model + MLA pool + encoder each
  • Around 5–6 streams the shared MLA pool can run out, and that failure kills every running stream, not just the new one

Capacity (30 fps)

Grouped

Independent groups of 4 streams

What it is

A middle ground: several independent detectors, each handling 4 streams. One model per group instead of per stream, and each group owns its own Insight channels.

Why use it

  • Changing a stream rebuilds only its own group of 4 — other groups keep running
  • Far cheaper than Live: 16 streams cost 4 detectors, not 16

Trade-off

  • A change still restarts the other 3 streams sharing that group

Capacity (30 fps)

Choosing quickly

Many fixed cameras → High-Scale. Cameras that come and go → Live-Add. Many cameras but you still want to edit one without disturbing the rest → Grouped.
